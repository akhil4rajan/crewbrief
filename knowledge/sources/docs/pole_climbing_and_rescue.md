# Pole climbing, pole testing and pole-top rescue

## Before climbing or de-tensioning

* Check the pole inspection tag. A red-tagged (condemned) or overdue pole must not be climbed.
* Sound and visually check the pole at the groundline for decay, termites, cracks and fire damage.
* Consider changes in loading: removing conductors or stays can make a sound pole fall. Support the pole
  with a crane, pole dollies or temporary stays before releasing tension.
* Where an EWP can reach, work from the EWP instead of climbing.

## Climbing

* Wear a full-body harness with pole strap; stay attached from the ground to the work position and
  back.
* Only one person on the pole at a time unless the task requires two and the pole is suitable.
* Carry tools in a bag and tether them; establish a drop zone at the base.

## Pole-top rescue

* A second worker trained in pole-top rescue is on site whenever someone is aloft.
* The rescue kit (rescue line, descender, sling) is at the base of the pole and has been checked.
* The rescue plan is discussed at the briefing: who rescues, how supply will be isolated if the casualty
  is in contact with conductors, who calls 000, and where the ambulance will be met (muster point).

## Pole replacement and lifting

* Prepare a lift plan: pole weight, radius, load chart, ground conditions and the set-up position.
* A licensed dogman slings the load and directs the crane; slings and rigging are inspected and in date.
* Control the pole with taglines. Nobody stands under or within the fall radius of a suspended pole.
* Where the crane or borer works near live overhead lines, keep outside the approach distance with a
  dedicated spotter and, where available, a proximity alarm or limiter.
