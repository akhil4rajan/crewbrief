# Confined spaces — pits, vaults and chambers

Cable pits, vaults, manholes and substation basements can contain low oxygen, hydrogen sulphide,
carbon monoxide or flammable gas, and can be difficult to escape from. Many large pits meet the
definition of a confined space under the WHS Regulations.

## Before opening

* Check the asset records and the asbestos register (older pits may be asbestos-cement).
* Open the lid with lifting aids. Look inside with a torch for snakes, spiders and water before reaching
  in.

## Before entry

* A confined space entry permit is issued after a risk assessment and isolation of any hazardous energy.
* Test the atmosphere from outside, at the top, middle and bottom, with a calibrated four-gas detector:
  oxygen 19.5–23.5 %, flammable gas below 5 % LEL, CO below 30 ppm, H2S below 10 ppm.
* Ventilate with a blower or venturi; keep it running during entry.

## During entry

* Monitor the atmosphere continuously; leave immediately on an alarm.
* A trained standby person remains at the entry, in constant contact, and never enters to perform a
  rescue.
* A rescue tripod and retrieval winch are rigged over vertical entries deeper than 1.5 m; the entrant
  wears a harness attached to the retrieval line.

## Rescue

The rescue plan is discussed at the briefing. Non-entry rescue (winch) is the default. Call 000 early.
