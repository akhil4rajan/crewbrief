# Heat, storms and bushfire weather

## Heat stress

Hot conditions combined with arc-rated clothing, heavy physical work and exposure to sun create a high
risk of heat illness.

* Check the forecast and plan heavy work for the cooler part of the day; start early on hot days.
* Drink water regularly (around 250 ml every 15–20 minutes) and use electrolytes on long hot days.
* Take scheduled rest breaks in shade or an air-conditioned vehicle; rotate tasks.
* Wear sun protection: long sleeves, wide brim, sunglasses and SPF 50+ sunscreen.
* Buddy-check each other. Confusion, stopping sweating, vomiting or collapse indicate heat stroke —
  call 000, cool the person and give first aid.

## Storms and lightning

* Check BOM warnings and radar before and during the job.
* Apply the 30/30 rule: stop work, come down from poles and EWPs and shelter in vehicles when the time
  between lightning and thunder is 30 seconds or less (within about 10 km); wait 30 minutes after the
  last thunder before resuming.
* Lower EWP baskets when wind exceeds the unit's limit (typically 12.5 m/s).
* During storm restoration treat every fallen conductor as live and keep the public at least 8 m away.

## Bushfire weather

* Check the fire danger rating and total fire ban status for the district each morning.
* On total fire ban days, hot work (grinding, welding, cutting) is done only under the network's
  exemption conditions with a fire watch, a charged extinguisher and water (knapsack) at the work point.
* Park vehicles off dry grass; exhausts can ignite it.
* On days rated Catastrophic, non-essential field work is postponed.
