# Traffic management for roadside works

Work on or beside a road requires a traffic guidance scheme (TGS) that suits the road speed, traffic
volume, sight distance and duration of the work, prepared and implemented by appropriately accredited
people in accordance with AS 1742.3 and the road authority's code of practice.

## Before setting up

* Confirm the traffic management plan (TMP) and TGS for the site, and whether a road occupancy licence
  is required (arterial roads, lane closures, peak periods).
* Check for school zones, bus stops, driveways and pedestrian routes that the set-up must keep open or
  divert.

## Set-up

* Park the work vehicle upstream of the work area as a buffer, with amber beacons and arrow board on.
  On high-speed roads use a truck-mounted attenuator (TMA).
* Place advance warning signs, cones and delineators in the order shown on the TGS. Lane closures and
  stop-slow control are done by accredited traffic controllers.
* Every worker wears day/night high-visibility clothing. Night work also needs lighting that does not
  dazzle drivers.

## Pedestrians and the public

Keep footpaths open or provide a protected detour. Use barriers or fencing, not just bunting, where
there is an open excavation or work at height over a footpath. A spotter is used where the public may
walk under work at height or near reversing plant.

## Mobile plant on site

Trucks and plant reverse only with a spotter or with a reversing camera and alarm, and only inside the
work zone. Keep people out of the swing and travel paths of plant.

## Pack-up

Remove devices in the reverse order of set-up. Never leave signs that no longer apply.
