# Electrical Safety Rules — field extract

This extract summarises the rules field crews apply on the distribution network. The full Electrical
Safety Rules and the applicable state regulations take precedence. Where this extract and the rules
differ, the rules apply.

## Treat everything as live

All conductors and apparatus are treated as live until they have been isolated, proven de-energised
and earthed in accordance with these rules. "It should be dead" is never an acceptable basis for work.
Customer solar inverters, home batteries and standby generators can backfeed an isolated network, so an
isolated LV circuit is still treated as live until every source of supply has been isolated and the
conductors have been tested dead at the work point.

## Isolation

* Identify every possible source of supply from the network diagram, the switching program and a site
  check (look for rooftop solar, inverters, batteries and generator changeover switches).
* Isolate at a defined point: open the switch, remove the fuses or open the links. Service fuses are
  removed and kept in the possession of the person doing the work.
* Lock out and attach a personal danger tag at every point of isolation. Only the person who attached a
  personal danger tag may remove it.
* HV isolation is carried out only by an authorised switching operator to an approved switching
  program. Points of isolation are locked and tagged and recorded on the program.

## Test before touch

1. Prove the tester on a known live source or proving unit.
2. Test every conductor (phase to phase, phase to neutral, phase to earth) at the work point.
3. Re-prove the tester.

A failed or doubtful test means stop: the conductors are treated as live.

## Earthing and short-circuiting

Portable earths are applied to HV conductors on all sides of the work after proving dead, and remain
in place until the work is complete and the access permit is surrendered. Earths are applied with an
earthing stick, earth end first. Where induced voltage from parallel or crossing circuits is possible,
apply earths at the work point and use equipotential bonding and an equipotential mat. Discharge cables
and capacitors through a rated discharge stick before earthing.

## Access permits

Work on or near isolated HV apparatus is carried out under an access permit issued by an authorised
person after isolation and earthing are complete. Every worker signs on to the permit at the briefing
and signs off when leaving the work area. The permit is surrendered only when all workers have signed
off and all earths and tools are clear.

## Minimum approach distances

People, tools and plant must stay outside the minimum approach distance (MAD) for the voltage unless
the conductors are isolated and earthed or the work is authorised live work:

| Voltage | Untrained person / plant (no observer) | Authorised person |
|---|---|---|
| LV insulated | 0.5 m | 0.5 m |
| LV bare | 1.0 m | 0.5 m |
| 11 kV / 22 kV | 3.0 m | 0.7 m |
| 33 kV | 3.0 m | 1.0 m |
| 66 kV | 3.0 m | 1.2 m |

Mobile plant working within 6.4 m of overhead lines requires a dedicated safety observer (spotter)
whose only task is to watch the clearances and stop the work.

## LV live work

LV live work is permitted only where isolation is impracticable and the job is on the approved live
work list. It requires insulating gloves with leather outers, arc-rated clothing and face shield,
insulated tools, insulating covers or mats on adjacent live parts, and a second person trained in LV
rescue standing by.

## Service connections and reconnections

Before energising or re-energising a service, carry out neutral integrity, polarity and insulation
resistance tests. A tingle from a tap or metal fixture indicates a possible neutral fault: keep people
clear and treat the installation as live.

## Switching and arc flash

Operate switchgear and drop-out fuses with an operating stick from the side, wearing arc-rated
clothing and face shield. Keep everyone not involved in the switching outside the exclusion zone.
